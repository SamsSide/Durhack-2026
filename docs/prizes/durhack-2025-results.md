## What this tells us

1. **No project won two prizes.** All 16 winning projects took exactly one prize each. Either the
   organisers spread prizes on purpose or it just fell that way; plan as if a team realistically
   wins one or two, and enter many to raise the odds of any.
2. **The big-value prizes are sponsor challenges, not MLH.** QRT paid 500 pounds per person, G-Research
   gave Switch 2s, Marshall Wace gave iPads. MLH prizes are small hardware. QRT's challenges were
   closed problems (their dataset, their sandbox) that could not be stacked with a general app.
3. **Open sponsor themes are loose on purpose** ("Bridging Worlds", "connect things that normally
   aren't"). Any project can be pitched into them; winners leaned into the theme literally and
   playfully (two crystal balls won two different prizes).
4. **Overall winners were physical, playful and instantly demo-able**: dance to make music; a
   book that makes its own soundscape. Neither was a CRUD app or a chatbot. Judges walk round
   150 tables; a demo that works on a passer-by in 20 seconds wins.
5. **There was a theme** ("Legacy": Legacy Luminary, Legacy FM, "your legacy is the knowledge you
   leave behind"). The 2026 site art is sky, sun, blimp and a city (Skyline, Plaza, Metro), so
   expect a theme announced at the opening and prize names that match it. Winners wove the theme
   in.
6. **MLH winners used the sponsor tech as the core of the experience**, not as a bolt-on:
   Legacy FM is a radio station (ElevenLabs is the product); Fanfiction rewrites books (Gemini is
   the product) and also used ElevenLabs.
7. **Solo and two-person teams won** (2nd place and Accenture were solo). Scope discipline beats
   headcount.
