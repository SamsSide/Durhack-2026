## P11 - Marshall Wace: "New Worlds"

### 1. What they want

- Brief: explore "New Worlds", open a door to the unexplored: new data, ideas or something novel. Any interpretation. "Come and chat to us." Side prize: best meme wins a duffel bag (#marshall-wace).
- Who: London hedge fund, Platinum sponsor. Its student-events page says it sponsors hackathons because students "may bring new perspectives and insights", names IC Hack, Hack Cambridge, Hack 'n Roll, DurHack, DivHacks, GUTs and Hack the Burgh, and stresses entrepreneurial spirit and innovative thinking ([MW student events](https://www.mwam.com/student-events)).
- Pattern: MW writes one short evocative phrase per event and rewards the most original reading. Seen: "Exploring a Chain of Events" (DurHack 2024, IC Hack 25), "Emergent Behaviour" (IC Hack 26), "Bridging Worlds" (DurHack 2025), "Giving Back Challenge" (Hack the Burgh VIII). Sources: search-listing of the Devpost prize pages for [IC Hack 25](https://ic-hack-25.devpost.com/), [IC Hack 26](https://ic-hack-26.devpost.com/), [DurHack 2024](https://durhack-2024.devpost.com/), [Hack the Burgh VIII](https://htb8.devpost.com/) (prize names via search snippets; pages not opened).
- What lights judges up (inference from the winner and the pattern): a literal, playful reading of the phrase, one tangible or visual "door" the judge can step through in 20 seconds, and a data angle (MW is a data and quant house). Unconfirmed: no rubric published.

### 3. Judging

- Who: MW staff on site (they ask you to come and chat). Names unconfirmed. DurHack 2024 listing shows Marshall Wace as a judge (search snippet).
- Format: table visit plus Devpost submission, unconfirmed. Talk to them early: the brief invites it, and it is how they learn your intent.

### 4. Effort and risk

- Qualify: 0 to 1 hour on top of any app, if you give it a "new world" framing in the Devpost text and a demo opener ("this opens a door to...").
- Compete: 4 to 8 hours: a visible "explore the unknown" mechanic (new dataset joined in, generated world, map with fog of war) with real, surprising data or output.
- Risks: vague theme means judges compare projects on the "wow", so a generic CRUD app with a theme sticker loses; the prize is not announced (iPads last year); ORION-style hardware is expensive in hours.

### 5. Stackability

- With Accenture and Waterstons: yes (see section 4 of this file, angles).
- With MLH: Gemini (generates the unexplored content), ElevenLabs (narrator for the new world), Tiger Data (new data stored and time-series explored), Vultr (host). All fit.
- Pitch angle: "We open a door to a world nobody has looked at: [new data joined in a way nobody has], explored live."

### 6. Scores

- Fit for any idea: 5 (a theme phrase can wrap almost anything).
- Win chance: 3 (one winner, loosely judged; originality is the whole game).
- Value: 4 (iPads last year; unconfirmed for 2026).
- Effort: 4 (framing is cheap; a real "wow" costs hours).

## P17 - Accenture: "The Accenture Challenge"

### 1. What they want

- Brief: find a frustrating process people accept as "just the way things work" and redesign it with modern AI and LLMs. Criteria (given): Problem and User Pain; Innovation and Reimagination; Effective use of AI and LLMs; Impact and Value; Prototype and Technical Execution; Responsible AI and Feasibility.
- Who: global consultancy. Wants consulting-style pitches: named user, quantified pain, redesigned flow, value, risk handling. Last year's brief added fairness, privacy and explainability ([DurHack 2025 Devpost](https://durhack-2025.devpost.com/), via durhack-2025-results.md).
- What lights judges up: a before-and-after of a real process (steps, time, errors), the LLM doing something a rules-based tool could not, and an honest responsible-AI slide (bias, privacy, human in the loop, failure modes). Inference from the six criteria; no further rubric found. (Accenture's responsible-AI page URL I tried returned 404, so no first-party wording was read.)

### 3. Judging

- Who: Accenture staff attending DurHack (names unconfirmed). Probably 1 to 2 consultants or technologists.
- Format: likely table or short demo against the six criteria; unconfirmed. Six explicit criteria suggest a scored rubric: write the Devpost text to hit each heading in order.

### 4. Effort and risk

- Qualify: 1 to 2 hours on top of an AI web app: pick a named process, write a one-slide before-and-after, add a responsible-AI note. Requires the app to attack a process, not just be "an AI tool".
- Compete: 4 to 6 hours: measure the old process (step count, time), show the new flow end to end, add a visible safeguard (confidence, human-approval step, data minimisation), and one honest limitation.
- Risks: "chatbot over documents" is the default and will look generic; claims of impact need numbers you can defend (invent none; use measured demo timings or cited stats); responsible-AI is scored, so skipping it costs a criterion.

### 5. Stackability

- With Marshall Wace: yes if the frustrating process is framed as an unexplored territory (the "door").
- With Waterstons: strong (processes are frustrating because systems are not connected; Waterstons is a systems-integration consultancy).
- With MLH: Gemini (the redesign engine), ElevenLabs (voice front-end for the redesigned process, good for accessibility), Tiger Data (log process runs and before/after timings as time-series), Vultr (host).
- Pitch angle: "[User] loses [N] minutes on [process] because nobody questioned it; we cut it to [M] with an LLM, with a human approval step and no personal data stored."

### 6. Scores

- Fit for any idea: 4 (needs a process framing).
- Win chance: 3 (small prize, solo won last year, rubric is explicit so a clear pitch beats a clever app).
- Value: 2 (Pi 5 and vouchers last year; unconfirmed for 2026).
- Effort: 4.

## Three project angles that satisfy all three briefs plus "Composition"

1. **Paperwork Atlas.** Pick one painful process (student housing deposit dispute, visa renewal, NHS referral; choose one the team has lived). Gemini reads public rules and your messy inputs, builds the process as a graph, finds the unused shortcuts, and produces a redesigned flow. The graph is rendered as an explorable map where unexplored shortcuts are fog-of-war "doors" (MW: new world). It connects the siloed bodies in the process (Waterstons). Each stage plays an instrument; completing the process composes a short piece (Composition). ElevenLabs narrates. Tiger Data stores before-and-after timings. Add human-in-the-loop and no-personal-data notes (Accenture).
2. **Silo Synth.** Connect two systems that never talk (campus timetable and room-booking data, or a tool's calendar and its chat) and compose a live soundtrack from the joined stream: conflicts are dissonance, free slots are consonance. Accenture: the redesigned process is "scheduling and catch-up", LLM proposes fixes. MW: a "new world" the data has never been heard as. Waterstons: two unconnected systems joined. Composition: the output is music. Voice via ElevenLabs.
3. **Stranger Songs.** Connect two people from unconnected communities (like SkillSwap, but the match produces a joint artwork or song from their interests, generated by Gemini and voiced by ElevenLabs). Accenture: redesign the awkward "how do I find a study partner or society" process, with a clear fairness and privacy note (opt-in profiles, explainable matches). MW: a new world made from two people's worlds. Waterstons: connecting people who would not meet. Composition: idea mixing and music.

Recommendation: angle 1 or 2 if the team has a data source ready; angle 3 is cheapest and lowest risk but closest to last year's SkillSwap. Whichever is chosen, have one measurable number (minutes saved, steps removed) for Accenture and one visual "door" for MW.
