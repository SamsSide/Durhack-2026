# DurHack 2026: predicted sponsor challenges

*Written 13 Nov 2026, the night before the event. The sponsor challenges are not public yet. This is a prediction built from DurHack 2024 and 2025, sponsor sites, and what could be reached. Devpost returned 403 or a bot wall for every fetch, and WebSearch found almost nothing about these sponsors at other UK hackathons. Treat the "other hackathons" evidence as thin. Anything not linked is unconfirmed.*

Last year's briefs and winners are in [durhack-2025-results.md](durhack-2025-results.md).

## How DurHack runs (from what is known)

- Format 2025: 24 hours, over 600 participants, a "legacy" theme and "bridging worlds" sponsor theme, sponsor "problem statements" such as best use of Gemini, ElevenLabs and AI on Arm, a "hackiest hack" prize, and guild side challenges (find the DurHuck Dino, act out an algorithm, build a model windmill). Source: [The Tab recap, 6 Nov 2025](https://thetab.com/2025/11/06/heres-everything-you-missed-at-durhack-x-this-year).
- DurHack 2024 (2-3 Nov 2024): Devpost prize pool 6,472 pounds, first prize "Grand Voyager", a Marshall Wace "Chain of events" challenge and a "Funniest Hack" prize, per search snippets of [the 2024 Devpost page](https://durhack-2024.devpost.com) (not opened directly).
- Judging style (table judging or stage finals): unconfirmed. Plan for judges visiting tables, with a Devpost submission needed for every challenge you want to enter.

## Marshall Wace (Platinum)

- Does: large systematic and discretionary hedge fund (London). Its tech pages stress in-house technology, data and quant research. Engineers there care about clean systems, data pipelines and "new perspectives" ([MW student events](https://mwam.com/student-events): "may bring new perspectives and insights into the industry").
- Hackathon history: sponsors IC Hack (title sponsor 2026, [Imperial news](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/news/articles/engineering/computing/2026/ic-hack-26-the-students-inventing-the-future/)), Hack Cambridge, Hack 'n Roll, DurHack, DivHacks, GUTs and HackTheBurgh (per MW's own page). IC Hack 26 used "sponsor- and organiser-led categories" with no single overall prize; MW's category brief there was not found.
- DurHack record: 2024 "Chain of events" (a theme to explore, one winner), 2025 "Bridging Worlds" (open theme, iPads). Both are one-line, loosely worded themes, not datasets.
- Prediction: open theme, a short evocative phrase tied to the weekend theme, judged on concept and creativity. Confidence high (about 85 percent) that it is open. Prize: hardware (iPads or similar), a few hundred pounds of value per person.
- What won before: ORION, a physical crystal orb that compares your data to odd datasets. Literal, playful interpretation of the phrase plus a physical object. Pitch: start the demo with the theme phrase, show something tangible.

## Qube Research & Technologies, QRT (Platinum)

- Does: quant hedge fund. Runs the QRT Academy and data challenges (a 2025 public data challenge on predicting blood-cancer risk, per [eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/the-unusual-hedge-fund-quant-challenge-with-no-trading-involved); no cash prize there). Engineers care about correct maths, reproducibility, performance and clean data handling. QRT also ran an Imperial SIF quant competition in 2025 (details not found).
- DurHack record: 2025, two closed challenges (security mini-CTF and AWS tasks in their sandbox; "Meeting in the Middle" fairest, lowest-carbon meeting place from OAG flight data), 500 / 250 / 150 pounds per person.
- Prediction: closed problem with their own data or sandbox, a self-contained puzzle with a leaderboard or score. Confidence medium-high (70 percent) it is closed again; 20 percent it is a new quant or data-science task; 10 percent open. Prize: cash per person, 150-500 pounds, the biggest in the room.
- Not stackable with a general AI app. Needs a separate small team or a person who owns it. Pre-think: a fast Python and Polars or pandas pipeline, a clear scoring metric, a README with assumptions, and a short written explanation of method (the 2025 winners shipped a tool the brief asked for, not an idea).

## BidFX (Platinum, new)

- Does: cloud FX execution platform for hedge funds, asset managers and banks: EMS, liquidity aggregation (spot, swaps, forwards), risk and compliance, transaction cost analysis, and a newer data and analytics product. Owned by SGX (Singapore Exchange) since 2020. Source: [The TRADE](https://www.thetradenews.com/sgx-to-acquire-remaining-stake-in-bidfx-for-128-million), [FinanceFeeds](https://financefeeds.com/sgxs-bidfx-launches-data-analytics-solution/). Its own domain redirects to SGX FX.
- Engineers care about: latency, reliability under load, real-time market data, clean UI for traders, FIX connectivity. (The last point is general FX knowledge, unconfirmed for BidFX.)
- Hackathon history: nothing found. First DurHack. The only related find was iFX Hack 2026 (AWS-powered, Limassol), with no BidFX link.
- Prediction: most likely an open fintech-flavoured challenge (best trading or market-data tool, best real-time dashboard, best use of live FX data) with their own demo; a small chance of a closed task using a market-data feed. Confidence low (45 percent open, 35 percent closed, 20 percent other). Prize: guess 100-300 pounds equivalent per person, or tech hardware.
- Pitch ideas: anything real-time and visual (live rates, volatility, cost-of-execution explained to a non-expert), plus a latency number on screen.

## Accenture (Gold)

- Does: global consultancy; applied AI, cloud, "responsible AI" language.
- DurHack record: 2025 "AI tool for a real everyday problem; must use AI, working demo, ethical AI (fairness, privacy, explainability)". Winner WalkSafe (solo, Svelte, Python, Gemini, OpenStreetMap). Prize Raspberry Pi 5 and 25 pound vouchers.
- Other UK hackathons: an old [Surrey news item](https://www.surrey.ac.uk/news/first-year-students-win-accenture-challenge-hackathon-old) shows an Accenture-sponsored challenge at HackCity 2018. Nothing for 2025-2026.
- Prediction: open theme again, very likely same shape (AI tool plus ethics). Confidence high (80 percent). Prize: small hardware or vouchers. Stackable with a general AI app: yes, if the app solves a real everyday problem and you add a fairness or privacy slide.
- What won: a practical safety app for students, solved by one person, working live. Pitch: name the user and the problem in one line, say how you handled bias, privacy and explanation.

## RS Group (Gold, new)

- Does: distributor of electronic, electrical and industrial components (RS Components, DesignSpark community, RS Online). Engineers care about prototyping, components, sustainability, "making"; DesignSpark runs community challenges ([Arduino UNO Q Challenge](https://www.industrialtechnology.co.uk/rs-designspark-announces-arduino-uno-q-community-challenge-winner), 5,000 dollar prize, June 2026).
- Hackathon history: backs HackNotts through the RS Student Fund and as a hardware source (components borrowed free from "a suitcase of microcontrollers and sensors"): [HackNotts 24](https://www.rs-online.com/designspark/hacknotts-24), [HackNotts 25](https://www.rs-online.com/designspark/planning-hacknotts-25). No RS-set challenge brief was found. RS also runs student funds paid in RS products (not cash).
- Prediction: a hardware challenge (best hardware hack, best use of embedded or IoT, possibly a sustainability angle), prize paid in RS components or a DesignSpark gift. Possibly lends hardware at the venue. Confidence medium (60 percent hardware or IoT open theme; 25 percent generic "best use of RS parts"; 15 percent something else). Prize: component or kit credit, about 50-150 pounds each.
- Stackable with a general AI app: only if the app has a hardware or sensor front end. Pre-think: a cheap sensor or camera plus a model, a physical output (motor, LED, display), shown live. Last year's physical winners (Clap Collider, Niche Machine, SMART TONGS) show the room likes this.

## Neptune North (Gold)

- Does: technology company founded 2024 as a joint venture of Oliver Wyman and Rokos Capital Management; builds custom technology for banking and financial services, private capital and defence; HQ Newcastle upon Tyne. Roles: software, data, ML, applied AI, DevOps, test automation, graduate programmes. Source: [Neptune North careers](https://job-boards.eu.greenhouse.io/neptunenorth/jobs/4894923101). (Do not confuse with the US "Neptune North LLC" consultancy site.) Rokos is a macro fund.
- DurHack record: 2025 "anticipate a macroeconomic trend from non-traditional, high-frequency data", prize 12-month Udemy each. Winner: inflation prediction with autoregression plus news sentiment, a notebook, Guardian API.
- Prediction: open theme in the same data-science and finance vein (forecasting from alternative data, or a "Rokos-style" macro signal), possibly a variation. Confidence medium-high (70 percent). Prize small (learning credits or vouchers).
- Stackable with a general AI app: partly. A prediction or signal feature, with clean data and a backtest or error metric, can be added to a data-flavoured project. Engineers there care about data quality, testing and reproducibility, so show tests and a clear metric.

## Waterstons (Gold, founding sponsor)

- Does: Durham-based "bigger picture technology consultancy": IT, data and AI, cyber, software, managed services, for energy, education, manufacturing, housing, private equity and logistics. Registered office in Aykley Heads, Durham. Source: [waterstons.com](https://www.waterstons.com).
- DurHack record: 2025 "Improve the world by connecting things that normally aren't" (systems, IoT, people, cultures), prize a Keybow mini keyboard; winner SkillSwap (skill bartering with match scores and chat, React, Supabase, Gemini). No other-hackathon challenge found.
- Prediction: open, loosely worded, practical-for-people theme, possibly echoing last year or the weekend theme. Confidence high (80 percent open). Prize: small hardware gadget, maybe 20-50 pounds each.
- Stackable: yes. What won: a useful, polished web app a judge could imagine real people using. Pitch: a real user, a real integration (several systems joined up), and a data or security note (they are a cyber and data house).

## Newton Consulting, newtonimpact.com (Silver, new)

- Does: strategic delivery and operational-improvement partner for government, defence, energy and utilities, health, housing, retail and transport. Quotes clear outcomes, such as a 100,000-case backlog cleared, 700 percent productivity rise, "flow" and efficiency language ([newtonimpact.com](https://www.newtonimpact.com)). Little on AI beyond an Enterprise AI Summit.
- Hackathon history: a "Newton" logo appears on the Hackathons UK site; not confirmed to be this company. No challenge found.
- Prediction: likely an open "improve a process" challenge (best tool that makes a real workflow faster, cheaper or less wasteful), or a sponsor mentor and judge with a small prize. Confidence low (40 percent open challenge; 40 percent judging only or no named challenge; 20 percent other). Prize: small (vouchers).
- Stackable: yes, if you frame the project with before-and-after numbers (minutes saved, cases cleared). Pitch in their language: bottleneck, flow, measurable impact.

## Tonight: ideas to pre-think

1. A physical, instantly demoable build (webcam, sensor, orb, radio) that also takes an AI core. It can enter Marshall Wace (theme), Waterstons (connects things), RS (hardware), Accenture (everyday problem) and the overall prize.
2. A separate small data lane for QRT and Neptune North, with Polars or pandas ready, since both are likely closed or data-flavoured and pay best per person.
3. A fintech visual (live FX or market data, explained simply) as an optional hook for BidFX once the brief is out.
4. Prepare a one-line pitch per sponsor language: outcome and numbers (Newton), connected systems (Waterstons), ethics and privacy (Accenture), reproducibility and tests (Neptune North, QRT).
